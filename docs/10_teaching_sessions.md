# 🎓 Microteaching

In this trainings, microteaching refers to a practical activity in which you prepare and deliver a short teaching session based on your **case study**. The purpose is not only to demonstrate technical knowledge, but also to apply the teaching principles, lesson-planning approaches, and facilitation methods introduced during the virtual preparation phase.

{: .important }
The aim of the activity is not to present your case study results again. It is to practice how to identify the essential learning points of a topic you have worked on and transform them into a clear and engaging teaching session for others.

## 🧩 Your topic

Your microteaching topic builds on your case study. You decide which part of your genome reconstruction workflow you want to teach. 
Choose a focused topic that fits the available teaching time. 

---

## 🛠️ Preparation period

You will have two days to prepare your microteaching session. During this period, you will need to:

1. Select the part of your case study you want to teach.
2. Define clear and realistic learning objectives.
3. Decide how the topic should be explained and demonstrated.
4. Use the 4C Map for instructional design & delivery to design the session.
5. Prepare suitable teaching materials, examples, exercises, or discussion questions.

You don't have to cover every technical detail. Focus on the information learners need in order to understand the topic and begin applying it.

---

## 🤝 Trainer consultations

Although most of the preparation will be self-directed, trainers will be available throughout the preparation days for consultations.

You may ask trainers for support with:

* identifying reliable documentation and learning resources
* defining appropriate learning objectives
* selecting the scope of the session
* designing exercises or demonstrations
* structuring the lesson
* resolving technical problems
* reviewing teaching materials

---

## 👩‍🏫 Delivery

On the final day of the workshop, you will deliver your microteaching session to the other participants.

The session should demonstrate both technical understanding and effective teaching practice, using the methods that were presented during the program. Aim to:

* follow the 4C Map from Connections, to Concepts, to Concrete practice, to Conclusions
* implement activities that apply the Six Trumps
* explain technical concepts in accessible language
* involve the learners through questions, discussion, or practical activities
* manage the available time effectively

The other participants will take the role of learners. This creates an opportunity to practice teaching in a supportive environment and to learn from the different approaches used by the group.

---

## 💬 Reflection and feedback

After each teaching session, you will reflect on your experience and receive constructive feedback from peers and trainers.

{: .discussion }
How did you decide which information from your case study was essential for your learners? What did you leave out, and why?

---

## 🎯 Learning outcomes

By the end of the microteaching block, you will be able to:

1. Formulate clear, measurable learning objectives for your session using action verbs from Bloom's taxonomy.
2. Design a short teaching session that follows the 4C Map and includes activities that apply the Six Trumps.
4. Explain a technical step of the genome reconstruction workflow in accessible language, using examples or a live demonstration.
5. Facilitate learner involvement through questions, discussion, or hands-on practice while keeping to the allotted time.
6. Reflect on your own teaching and give and receive constructive feedback.
